## Replacement direction and stage2.1 scope

Audience/task: the owner and Mimi; a real personal question, physical card selection, reflection and a later revisit. Desktop pointer/keyboard is the primary surface. AI and cross-device saving are deferred. Product structure and exact reading gestures remain to be refined before replacing the legacy board.

Selected emotional direction: a quiet, immersive divination ritual with a dark tactile table, strongly legible card thickness, restrained warm light and meaningful reveal rhythm. The actual card action is the composition's focus; controls remain peripheral and understandable. Reference 占星猫 for the bottom spread/table relationship, not its shuffle animation. Primary visual evidence: https://app.mi.com/details?id=com.starcatzx.starcat . Static references do not establish specific gestures.

The stage2.1 study now tests a continuous airborne reset gesture lasting about 5.06 seconds: cards fly in slowly on varied trajectories, pass through a loose suspended arrangement without stopping, then progressively accelerate into an aligned rectangular stack. The 2.6-second prelude joins the existing choreography at its already-lifted stack (original progress 0.18), omitting the old stationary/table-lift opening. Splitting, held bends, interleaving, recoil, settling and spreading then run at their original speed for 2.46 seconds. The earlier table landing and scatter pause are superseded. The camera stays still; introductory text yields the table to the cards during the prelude.

Three clearly labelled face-up sample cards demonstrate continuity from an earlier reading: they lift from their visible positions and turn back-up. The other 75 cards enter from four offscreen edges. This is an illustrative fixture, not a functional reading or saved data. Each physical card keeps its identity; one prepared run fixes both the randomized slot order and its seeded movement. Scrubbing does not redraw either. The completion button explicitly replays from the sample spread. Trial 02's held inner-edge bend, short small recoil, shared face/edge/shadow deformation and original timings are retained. Stable view presets, pause/scrub and slow replay aid evaluation.

Trial surface/state contract, selected under the start request: desktop windows around 1024–1600 px wide; narrower windows retain usable review controls but carry no phone gameplay acceptance. Pointer and keyboard operate native labelled buttons and timeline; focus is visible. Initial state is still, playback starts by action, running state prevents duplicate starts, skip/reduced motion settles at the same final pose, and unavailable WebGL displays a readable error. No automatic looping, moving camera, required audio, or hover-only controls. The shuffle effect is accepted as the local baseline; the full reading interface and remaining visual treatment are still candidates.

Materials/art: original procedural card-back linework, paper-colored edges, dark woven table, warm/cool light separation. These are trial choices within the selected atmosphere, not a settled brand system. The study uses Three.js in its isolated package; no renderer is yet mandated for the production application.

## stage2.2 — local interaction contract

Source: the user explicitly authorized free placement, dragging from the lower deck and a complete three-card draw/place/reveal/reset trial. The subsequent asynchronous answer selects **double-click to reveal**, superseding the earlier selected-card-button proposal as the primary gesture. A visible Reveal button remains a keyboard alternative. This is local trial 04 at reading.html; the accepted trial 03 page and its 12 implementation/test files remain untouched and reachable at the original index page.

The whole table is the primary surface. The lower face-down fan retains every remaining card's original slot. Revision 9 replaces uniform spacing/angle with a stronger arc, bounded uneven spacing, depth and yaw, following the supplied screenshot's loose overlapping arrangement. One layout is derived from the prepared order and remains fixed through hover, draw, return and undo; only a new shuffle prepares a new layout. Pointer-local separation helps inspection. Stable card-plane hit zones follow the camera while ignoring hover displacement. In response to the user’s attached hover screenshot, the candidate keeps its existing angle/depth, lifts only slightly, and neighbors ease apart and return smoothly; the full-card pop-out is superseded. Drag onto the table to draw, drag a placed card to move, single-click to select and raise it, double-click to reveal in place. A drag never counts as either reveal click; cancellation, Escape or an invalid drop returns to the prior state. A hidden card may return to its original slot. Revealed identity/knowledge cannot be undone into a fresh concealed card. Undo restores drawing/placement actions where knowledge permits; optional three-card arrangement moves the first three drawn cards as one operation. The deck is 78 cards, not a hard three-card limit.

A plain finite fabric rectangle supplies visible edges, restrained weave, a quiet hem and static edge folds. No occult symbols or decorative cloth design is introduced. It stays attached to the world/table while the camera moves, providing a spatial reference; improved comfort is an intention requiring user experience, not an established outcome. Revision 10 enlarges it from 12.4 × 9.2 to 14.8 × 11.2 scene units, centered at z = −0.6 so the front edge stays fixed. Full cards may be placed with centers at x ±6.6 and z −4.6 to 0.75, expanding the sides/back while leaving the fan and card dimensions unchanged.

Blank-table primary dragging changes elevation (34–60 degrees) and horizontal direction (±18 degrees); card gestures take priority. Direct view movement follows the pointer without momentum; downward dragging now raises the viewing elevation, reversing revision 7 at the user’s request. Horizontal direction is unchanged. Shift + arrow keys offer the same bounded view adjustments, and Restore View eases back to the original angle and 100% scale. Wheel-up brings the central table closer and wheel-down widens the overview, bounded to 75–180% with short smoothing. Native minus/plus buttons and keys offer equivalent zoom. Close zoom may crop peripheral cards; zooming out restores the overview. Wheel gestures with browser-zoom modifiers remain browser-owned, and zoom pauses during card/view dragging or shuffle. The camera stays above the table; framing accommodates the allowed card area at the view limits. Escape, capture loss and resize cancel unfinished gestures. At 100%, home/orbit framing fits the enlarged conservative card area. Reset first eases for 320ms to the original shuffle angles, scale and framing, then starts the retained clock. Its final 520ms returns to the enlarged reading frame along with the fan; reduced motion and skip finish immediately in that enlarged home view. The accepted 0.085 hover lift is unchanged; neighbor displacement increases from 0.18 to 0.32 scene units with the same easing.

Identity and reversal are fixed for the reading. New shuffle prepares one new order and orientation array once; animation, seeking, moving and undo never reroll them. Each reveal generates a distinct original numbered sample face on demand. These are labelled illustrative cards, not authored tarot interpretations or a license decision about the old deck art. Reversal is independent of drag position. Revision 11 reveals around the card’s long axis, preserving its printed top/bottom direction. Choose left or right once at the start of each reveal, independently of identity and reversal; retries, animation frames and movement do not reroll either. Both turn directions finish at the same pose for a given reversal. The 620ms gesture lifts by the short-edge clearance, turns sideways and settles at the original table position. Double-click, the Reveal button and Enter share the motion; reduced motion immediately shows the same final orientation. A revealed card stays face-up while dragged. Reset can continue from an interrupted side turn through the existing actual-pose adapter. Flip clearance and final face-up thickness retain legible layered paper solids.

The accepted airborne shuffle is reused through a reading-only reset adapter. It starts at actual rendered table poses, including an interrupted reveal. The remaining fan leaves as one rigid group before its order is changed offscreen; the existing choreography then returns the placed cards from their positions and brings the remaining cards in from the edges. This is authored screen choreography, not a continuous physical simulation of hidden cards. After the accepted final fan, a short positioning transition lowers the deck into the selectable strip. New-round state becomes interactive only on completion or explicit skip. Reduced motion completes immediately.

Desktop pointer/keyboard remains primary. Native previous/next/place controls and placed-card buttons provide non-drag access; no action requires hover alone. Enter operates a focused table selection, Escape cancels dragging; controls show disabled and selected states. Empty/partially drawn/partially revealed/complete/resetting/error states have visible guidance. Resizing cancels unfinished dragging, and the drawing area stays recoverable. Phone polish, saving, meanings, questions and AI remain outside this slice. The action sequence is agreed; usability and visual acceptance require the user's trial.
